AI-Assisted Walking Cane

An assistive robotics prototype using Raspberry Pi, a camera, and YOLO-based computer vision to explore environment awareness for visually impaired users.

Why this project matters

Navigation support for people with visual impairments is a meaningful application of embedded intelligence. The project investigated how affordable computing hardware and computer vision can contribute to awareness of nearby objects.

System approach

The prototype combines a Raspberry Pi embedded computer, a camera for visual input, and a YOLO-based object-detection workflow. The central engineering task was bringing visual sensing and on-device computation into a portable assistive concept.

This is an exploratory research prototype—not a certified mobility aid or an autonomous navigation system.

Development & demonstration

Work involved developing and demonstrating a computer-vision-based walking cane concept through the Applied Engineering and Innovation Research Group. The prototype was showcased at the NCC Expo in Abuja, presenting how AI can connect with physical assistive technology.

Engineering considerations

For a real deployment, reliable detection would not be sufficient on its own. Latency, power consumption, positioning, environmental conditions, feedback to the user, and comprehensive safety testing would all require careful validation.

Next iteration

Measure detection speed and power usage on-device; test in varied environments; assess the usability of feedback methods with intended users; and conduct supervised field testing with appropriate safeguards.
